# Bulk research deployment

The shortlist's **Run research on selected** action queues up to 200 shows. It uses saved research prompts and generates the recommended angle's pitch sequence. Completed research, archived shows, active runs, and shows requiring relationship review are skipped. It does not replace campaign drafts or send emails. Generated sequences are stored in the batch and available under **View draft**.

## Deployment

1. Apply `20261031000500_bulk_research.sql`, then `20261031001000_parallel_bulk_research.sql` `20261031001100_research_queue_refill.sql`, and `20261031001200_research_concurrency_ten.sql`.
2. Set a randomly generated `RESEARCH_TICK_SECRET` for the edge functions.
3. Deploy `workspace-client-shortlist` and `client-research-tick` with the checked-in JWT settings.
4. Add Vault secrets `research_tick_url` (the full URL of the `client-research-tick` function) and `research_tick_secret` (the same secret as the edge environment). Use the secure deployment secret mechanism; do not commit the values.
5. Deploy the frontend. Verify one selected show reaches research, pitch, and completed, and its saved draft can be opened after a page reload.

The initial migration installs a one-minute cron; the refill migration shortens it to 15 seconds. Each tick atomically claims up to ten jobs and processes them concurrently. The database enforces at most ten running jobs per workspace and twenty globally, including overlapping ticks. Each podcast retains its exclusive lease and sequential stages. Jobs that yield go to the back of the queue. Research continuation tokens persist across ticks. Workers revalidate the initiating user's current access, original credential epoch, subscription, and each show's relationship restrictions. No user bearer token is stored.

A worker that stops reporting for ten minutes becomes failed and requires an explicit retry. Completed stage outputs remain in the existing research document. Retry resumes that job's phase; a pitch failure does not rerun research. Batch results remain separate from existing campaign drafts. The batch-history selector retains previews from the most recent 1,000 jobs. Job transitions refresh the shortlist. The pitch editor reuses a completed batch draft when its research run still matches the current report, preserving existing editor drafts. Each batch invocation performs at most one new model call; pitch outputs checkpoint before yielding, and completed research is reconciled by run identity after a lost response. The executor saves the final pitch before responding, so a lost response does not downgrade completed work. Model calls share a 110-second invocation deadline within the worker’s 140-second timeout.

## Bulk pitch writing — 2026-11-15

**Write pitches for selected** queues a pitch-only batch on the same queue: `research-batch-start` with `mode: 'pitch'` inserts jobs that start in the pitch phase (`kind = 'pitch'`), so the worker, leases, concurrency caps, checkpointing, charging and refunds are unchanged. Shows are skipped with a reason when research is not complete, when the campaign already holds a pitch for them, when they are already queued, when a draft from the current research run already exists, or when they are archived or need relationship review. Nothing is added to a campaign and nothing is sent; the pitch view reuses the draft when it is opened, unless the chosen recipient is a producer or booking contact, which rewrites the greeting.

The shortlist polls `research-batch-list` with `slim: true`, which returns no email bodies or saved model outputs (the step count comes from the generated `pitch_steps` column); **View draft** reads one job through `research-batch-draft`. Polling is every 5 seconds while a job is queued or running and every 30 seconds otherwise, and the steps of a pitch-only job do not reload the shortlist. Batch start reads the whole selection's relationships in one lookup.

Apply `20261115000100_bulk_pitch_writing.sql` **before** deploying `workspace-client-shortlist`: the batch list reads the new `kind` and `pitch_steps` columns.

## Follow-up layout and bulk rewrite — 2026-11-16

`write_email` and `clean_email` now lay out both follow-ups as short emails: the greeting on its own line, a blank line, then two short paragraphs, with no restated credentials and no stock opener (follow-up one 45-75 words, follow-up two 30-55). `_shared/pitchFollowUps.ts` checks the layout in code, so a breach goes through the revision pass and anything left is shown as a flag. `PITCH_CHAIN_VERSION` is `p6-follow-up-layout-2026-11-16`. A workspace or client that saved its own `write_email` keeps its wording; the code check still applies.

**Rewrite drafts** (`research-batch-start` with `mode: 'pitch', rewrite: true`) writes a show's batch draft again with the current prompts. It only takes shows that have a draft and no copy on the campaign; the newest draft is the one the pitch view opens.

## Host name in the greeting — 2026-11-16

The claim audit was flagging "Hey Jonathan," as unsupported whenever the research report said the host's name was unconfirmed, and the revision pass then replaced it with "Hi there,".

Who to greet is the pitch writer's decision; code decides nothing. `_shared/pitchGreeting.ts` gives the writer a `<greeting>` block in the pitch context: the host record and whether an operator typed it or the directory supplied it, the publisher, the guests of recent episodes, the address the email goes to, and the cautions that matter (directories list a recent guest beside the host, a publisher is often a company, a wrong name is worse than none). It also offers one reading of the record as a suggestion the writer may overrule. The audit leaves the greeting to the writer and lists it only when it greets a known guest; `clean_email` is told to keep the greeting line. The only thing code does is `keepWriterGreeting`: when a revision replaces a name the writer chose with a nameless greeting, the writer's line is put back. `PITCH_CHAIN_VERSION` is `p7-writer-greeting-2026-11-16`.
